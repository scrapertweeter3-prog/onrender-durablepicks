Every product dies a particular way

Reviews that score at first touch miss the only question that matters ten years out. DurablePicks ranks by failure mode: work pants sorted by how they give out, buy-it-for-life mugs ranked by how they die, espresso machines judged on boiler metallurgy and whether an E61 group can actually be serviced in twenty years. The ranking is a survival curve, not a star rating.

Cost per decade, not cost at checkout

The site's unit of account is price divided by years of service. Lifetime-guaranteed socks from Darn Tough, Smartwool, and Icebreaker get compared by real cost per decade; heirloom leather duffels from Saddleback, Filson, and Clegg get ranked on 30-year survival; airless and HVLP paint sprayers get matched against each other on 20-year duty. A cheap tool bought twice is the expensive one, and the arithmetic here shows which is which.

The warranty is part of the tool

Fine print gets read like a spec sheet on durablepicks.com: what a lifetime guarantee actually covers, what voids it, and whether repairability beats replacement when the warranty runs out. Household gear, carry, kitchen, and apparel all run through the same failure-first method.
